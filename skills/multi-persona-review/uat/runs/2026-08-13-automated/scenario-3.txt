## What this review will not buy you, once it runs

- **Coverage, not accuracy.** Role personas do not improve factual accuracy — 162 personas over 2,410 questions, replicated on six models, nine significant *negative* differences. What holds up is coverage, and only through genuine differentiation; measured accuracy peaks at three to four differentiated roles.
- **Agreement is not confirmation.** One base model across all seats means correlated errors. If five seats agree the chart is fine, that is one opinion five times.
- **No user research.** A seat can analyse comprehension-test notes. It cannot *be* six readers.
- Accuracy comes from the verification pass against your source data — not from the panel.

## Evidence plan — the deliverable

Hand over these six, and the panel runs immediately.

1. **The artifact as published.** The image file (PNG/SVG/PDF), at delivered resolution. If it is an interactive or HTML chart, the page or a URL plus the rendered state. Acceptance: every number and label in it is legible at the size a reader receives it.
2. **The source data.** CSV/XLSX of the exact series plotted — not the upstream database, the rows that became the marks. Include: units, denominators, collection period, sample size (if survey), margin of error or CI, and the publisher/citation of the original statistic. Acceptance: I can recompute every plotted value and every number in the copy from this file alone. This is what makes step 5 (verify the decisive claims) possible; without it the review is opinion about a picture.
3. **The copy, as text.** Headline, standfirst, all annotations, axis and legend labels, footnotes, source line, date. Plain text or the layout file — not retyped from the image.
4. **Purpose and audience, in two sentences.** What one thing should a reader leave with, and who is the reader (general public / press / policy). This decides whether a defect is a defect.
5. **Surface and constraints.** Where it publishes, whether it must meet a conformance target (WCAG 2.1/2.2 AA, EN 301 549, a national public-sector accessibility rule), and any fixed template or brand constraints the panel must treat as given rather than attack.
6. **Decision owner, by name.** Who accepts, rejects, or defers each finding, and who applies the v2. Absent this, iteration 2 produces an assignment table with empty cells, which is an opinion wearing a schedule.

Optional but changes what can be claimed: any comprehension testing, screen-reader test output, or reader feedback. Supply it and user-outcome findings become available; withhold it and every reader-comprehension observation stays tagged `[INFERRED]`.
